📚 Administering a Running Record and Determining Reading Levels

A comprehensive guide to assessing student reading abilities

What is a Running Record?

A running record is an observational assessment tool used by educators to document a student's oral reading. It provides a detailed account of a student's reading behaviors, including miscues (errors), self-corrections, and fluency. Unlike standardized tests, a running record offers a real-time snapshot of a student's reading process, allowing teachers to identify specific areas of strength and weakness.

Running records are most effective when administered regularly to track progress over time and inform instructional decisions.

Preparing for a Running Record

1. Select an Appropriate Text ▼

Choose a text that is:

  • Unfamiliar to the student
  • At their estimated reading level
  • 100-200 words for younger readers, longer for older readers
  • A complete passage, rather than an excerpt
2. Gather Your Materials ▼

You will need:

  • The selected text
  • A running record form or blank sheet of paper
  • A pen or pencil
  • A timer (optional, for measuring reading rate)
3. Explain the Process to the Student ▼

Briefly explain to the student that you will be listening to them read and marking down what they say. Reassure them that this is not a test, but a way to help them become a better reader.

Running Record Symbols

During the running record, you'll use standardized symbols to record the student's reading behaviors:

✓ (Checkmark) The student reads the word correctly.
M (Miscue) The student makes an error. Write the word the student said above the actual word in the text. (Counts as error)
^ (Insertion) The student adds a word that is not in the text. Write the inserted word above the line. (Counts as error)
∅ (Omission) The student skips a word. (Counts as error)
T (Told) The student struggles with a word, and you provide it for them. (Counts as error)
SC (Self-Correction) The student initially makes an error but corrects it independently. (Does NOT count as error)
R (Repetition) The student repeats a word or phrase. (Does NOT count as error)

Administration Procedure

The Running Record Cycle

1
Observe

Watch and note student's reading actions

2
Record

Use standardized symbols to document

3
Analyze

Identify error patterns

4
Assess

Check comprehension and fluency

5
Determine

Calculate reading level

Key Steps:

  1. Have the student read aloud. Mark miscues and behaviors using the symbols. Set a timer for one minute and mark where they read to calculate words correct per minute (wcpm).
  2. Avoid interrupting. Allow students to decode independently. Only provide assistance if truly stuck.
  3. Note reading behaviors. Observe fluency, phrasing, and expression.
  4. Use two copies. Mark the teacher copy with all observations.

Calculating Accuracy Rate

Formula:
Accuracy Rate = (Total Words Read - Total Errors) ÷ Total Words Read × 100

Determining Reading Levels

Use the accuracy rate and comprehension observations to determine the student's reading level:

🟢 Independent Level

Accuracy Rate: 95-100%

Comprehension: Excellent - answers questions accurately and demonstrates strong understanding

What it means: The student can read the text fluently and accurately with excellent comprehension.

🟡 Instructional Level

Accuracy Rate: 90-94%

Comprehension: Good - answers most questions accurately with some prompting

What it means: The student can read with support and guidance. This is the ideal level for instruction.

🔴 Frustration Level

Accuracy Rate: Below 90%

Comprehension: Poor - struggles to answer questions and demonstrates limited understanding

What it means: The text is too difficult. The student struggles with decoding and comprehension.

Assessing Comprehension

Comprehension is just as important as accuracy. After the student reads, assess understanding through:

Retelling ▼

Ask the student to retell the story in their own words. This assesses their ability to recall key events and details.

Comprehension Questions ▼

Ask specific questions about the text, including:

  • Literal: Explicitly stated in the text
  • Inferential: Requiring the reader to draw conclusions
  • Evaluative: Requiring the reader to form opinions
Discussion ▼

Engage the student in a discussion about the text. This allows you to assess their understanding of themes, characters, and the overall message.

Using the Information

The information gathered from a running record can be used to:

  • Select appropriate reading materials: Choose books at the student's instructional level for guided reading and independent practice.
  • Plan targeted instruction: Focus on specific areas of weakness identified through miscue analysis.
  • Monitor progress: Administer running records regularly to track the student's growth over time.
  • Communicate with parents: Share the results with parents to provide them with a clear understanding of their child's reading abilities.

Conclusion

Administering a running record is a powerful tool for assessing a student's reading abilities and informing instruction. By carefully observing and recording a student's reading behaviors, calculating the accuracy rate, and assessing comprehension, educators can gain valuable insights into their strengths and weaknesses. This information can then be used to select appropriate reading materials, plan targeted instruction, and monitor progress, ultimately helping students become more confident and proficient readers.
